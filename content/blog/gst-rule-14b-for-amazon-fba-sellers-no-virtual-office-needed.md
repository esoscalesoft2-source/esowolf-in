GST Rule 14B is good news for Amazon FBA sellers who pay rent for virtual offices in other states just to hold a GST registration. From 1 November 2026, eligible sellers can register GST in another state using Amazon's fulfilment centre as their principal place of business. No virtual office and no rent agreement are needed.

This guide explains what the rule says, who qualifies, how to apply on the GST portal step by step, what limits apply, and how to close an old registration you no longer need.

> **Last checked: 10 November 2026** against the official notification and the GSTN advisory. This article is for general information. GST rules and portal steps can change, so please confirm with a qualified CA or tax professional before you file.

## What Is GST Rule 14B?

Rule 14B was added to the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Rules, 2017 through CBIC Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax, dated 26 October 2026. It came into force on 1 November 2026, after the GST Council recommended a simpler registration route for small online sellers at its 57th meeting.

The rule allows a seller of goods who sells through an e-commerce operator to register in another state by declaring the operator's warehouse as the principal place of business (PPoB). You do not need your own office, shop or virtual address in that state.

## Why This Matters for Amazon FBA Sellers

Under GST law, when your stock sits in a warehouse in another state, you have a place of business there. That means you need a GST registration in that state too. Until now, many FBA sellers rented a virtual office in each state only to show an address for this registration. That meant yearly rent, rent agreements and extra paperwork in every state.

With Rule 14B, Amazon's fulfilment centre in that state can be your registered address. You still have to file returns for the new registration, but you no longer pay rent for an office you never use. For a small seller who wants faster delivery across India through FBA, this makes expanding to more states much simpler.

## Eligibility: Who Can Use Rule 14B

You must meet all of these conditions:

| Condition | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Home-state registration | You already have an active regular GST registration in your home state, where you have a real physical place of business (office, shop or godown) |
| Same PAN | The new registration must be on the same PAN as your home-state GSTIN |
| No presence in the new state | In the state you are applying for, you have no office, shop or godown of your own and no active regular GST registration. Your only presence there is your stock in the e-commerce warehouse |
| Goods sold only through e-commerce | You sell goods only through operators that collect TCS under Section 52 (for example Amazon) |
| B2B tax limit | Your output tax on supplies to registered buyers (CGST + SGST + IGST together) is not more than ₹2.5 lakh per month; supplies between your own GSTINs on the same PAN are not counted |
| Operator consent | Amazon must approve the warehouse address on the GST portal within 7 working days |
| Aadhaar | Biometric Aadhaar authentication and document verification are needed. If you already did them for your home-state GSTIN, you do not repeat them. If not, complete them within 15 days of submitting Part B |
| One per state | Only one Rule 14B registration per PAN in each state |

### Documents you need

The good news is that the list is short:

- your home-state GSTIN (same PAN)
- Amazon's GSTIN for the state you are applying in (you can find it on Amazon's fee invoices or by searching on the GST portal)
- Aadhaar for biometric authentication, if you have not already done it for your home-state GSTIN

You do not need a rent agreement or NOC for the warehouse. If you are still setting up your seller account, our guide to [Amazon seller account setup](/blog/amazon-seller-account-setup/) covers the basics first.

## How to Apply on the GST Portal: Step by Step

These steps are based on the GSTN advisory of 1 November 2026. The portal screens may look slightly different, so follow the latest advisory on gst.gov.in.

### Step 1: Start a new registration

Log in at gst.gov.in, go to Services → Registration → New Registration, and choose the state you want to register in.

### Step 2: Open Part B and choose Rule 14B

Select your taxpayer category and open Part B of FORM GST REG-01. In the Business Details tab, select "Yes" for the option to register under Rule 14B and accept the declaration.

### Step 3: Enter your home-state GSTIN

Enter the GSTIN of your home state. It must be on the same PAN. The reason for registration is filled as selling through an e-commerce portal.

### Step 4: Add Amazon's GSTIN and request the address

In the place of business section, enter Amazon's GSTIN for that state. A request to use the warehouse address goes to Amazon. Once Amazon approves it, select the fulfilment centre as your principal place of business. You can add other Amazon warehouses in the same state as additional places of business.

### Step 5: Submit and complete verification

Fill in the remaining details and submit. If you have not done biometric Aadhaar authentication and document verification for your home-state GSTIN, complete it within 15 days of submitting Part B. Without it, the ARN is not generated.

## What Happens After You Apply

- **Amazon's consent:** Amazon has 7 working days to approve the address. If it does not, the application is not processed further.
- **Field visit:** An officer may visit your home-state premises. The application is deemed approved after the visit or after 30 days from the ARN, whichever is earlier.
- **Your certificate:** The registration certificate is issued electronically.

Once you have the new GSTIN:

1. Add it in Seller Central under your tax settings, then create FBA shipments to fulfilment centres in that state.
2. File separate returns for the new GSTIN.
3. Keep your books of account at your home-state principal place of business.
4. If Amazon ever withdraws its consent, change your principal place of business through FORM GST REG-14 within 30 days, or the registration is cancelled automatically.

Before you plan where to send stock, check which fulfilment centres are available to you and their exact addresses in Seller Central.

## Limits to Know Before You Apply

- You cannot apply if you already have an active GST registration in that state.
- A Rule 14B registration is only for sales through e-commerce operators, not for your own website or offline sales.
- Only one Rule 14B registration per state per PAN.
- The registration depends on the e-commerce operator's continued consent.
- The ₹2.5 lakh per month output tax limit on B2B supplies applies.

## How to Close an Old Virtual Office GST Registration

If you have an old GSTIN based on a virtual office and want to move to Rule 14B, you first need to close the old registration. Many sellers file this themselves on the portal:

1. File all pending returns (GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B) up to the latest period.
2. Check your Electronic Liability Register under Services → Ledgers and make sure nothing is pending.
3. Go to Services → Registration → Application for Cancellation of Registration.
4. Choose the reason that fits, such as discontinuance of business in that state, enter the date and submit.
5. After the tax officer approves the cancellation, file GSTR-10 (the final return) within three months of the cancellation order.

Do not apply under Rule 14B for the same state until the old cancellation is fully approved and closed. Until then, the old GSTIN still counts as an active registration in that state, and Rule 14B is only for states where you have no active registration — so an early application can be rejected. If you are unsure about stock, input tax credit or pending dues, speak to your CA first.
