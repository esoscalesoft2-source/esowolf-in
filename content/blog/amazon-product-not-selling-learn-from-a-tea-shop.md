Is your Amazon product not selling, even though your listing is live? You are not alone. Most new sellers face the same problem in the first weeks. Our founder Krishna M V explains the reason with a simple story: opening a tea shop in a busy market. Once you see your Amazon listing as a shop on a crowded street, it becomes clear why sales don't come on day one, and what you must do about it.

Krishna has 10+ years of hands-on experience on Amazon. He believes a small shop can grow into a very big business by selling on Amazon, but only when it follows the right steps. Here is the full lesson from his video, step by step.

## The tea shop story: why day one has no sales

Imagine you start a tea business in a busy market. On day one, you will not make many sales. Nobody knows your shop yet. There are many other tea stalls on the same street, and people walk past the ones they already know.

Launching a product on Amazon works the same way. Amazon is a very busy market. When a buyer searches for your product, your listing is just one of many similar products on the screen. So a new listing will not give you good sales by itself.

The question is: if your tea shop is not making sales, what will you do?

## Step 1: Make your shop (and your listing) stand out

A real tea shop owner would not just wait. They would do things to attract customers. They would put up a banner, polish the shop, hang lights and colourful decorations — anything that catches people's attention so the shop gets noticed.

Your Amazon listing needs the same kind of "garnishing".

**Improve your main image.** The main image is the first thing buyers see in search results. Tweak it so it looks a little different and better than the products around it. A clean, bright, clear image makes people stop and click on your product.

**Make your product page convincing.** When people land on your product page, the "About this item" section must give them reasons to buy. Add:

- the real pain point your product solves
- why the buyer needs this product
- what makes it special, for example who already loves it

Once your listing is ready, more people will notice it, click on it and start getting attracted to your product. In the video, the "people visiting your shop" line starts to rise only after these changes.

## Step 2: Test with friends, family and relatives

After decorating the new tea shop, what does the owner do next? They call their friends: "Come to my shop, I opened a new tea shop." Let's say 10 friends come and taste the tea. They will give honest inputs, like this:

| What friends say | What it tells you | What to fix |
|---|---|---|
| "This tea is good" | Something is working | Keep it the same |
| "The sugar is less" | One detail is off | Adjust the recipe |
| "This tea is bad" | The product has a real problem | Fix the product before you spend on ads |
| "Quality can be better" | Expectations are higher | Improve the quality, then test again |

Now you know what you are doing wrong and what you can improve in your product. That is the real value of testing.

Do the same with your Amazon product. Find your testers: friends, family, relatives — everyone you can reach. Ask them to look at your listing and use your product, and to tell you honestly what they think of the image, the title, the price and the product itself. Make a fix list, apply the changes, and test again.

**An important rule:** use your friends and family only for private feedback. Amazon does not allow reviews from friends, family or anyone connected to the seller, and breaking this rule can put your account at risk. Honest feedback before launch is helpful. Fake or "friendly" reviews are not.

## Step 3: Pick a niche and build a brand

Krishna ends the video with a simple example. In the Apple store, they don't sell apples. They sell electronic gadgets, and everyone knows exactly what to expect when they walk in.

Likewise, in your store, don't sell random products. Pick a niche and make it a brand. A tea shop that is famous for one special tea is easier to remember than a shop that sells a little of everything. On Amazon, a focused store builds trust, gets repeat buyers and makes every new product easier to sell.

## Quick checklist: is your listing ready?

Before you say "I have no sales on Amazon", ask yourself these questions:

1. Does my main image look better than the products around it in search?
2. Does "About this item" clearly explain the pain point and why the buyer needs my product?
3. Have I shown the product and listing to at least 10 people and collected honest feedback?
4. Did I fix the problems they found and test again?
5. Am I building one clear niche brand, or selling random products?

If you answered "no" to any of these, that is where your next sales are hiding.
