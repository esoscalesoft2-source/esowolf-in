How Much Investment to Start on Amazon? The 50% Rule

How much investment to start on Amazon do you really need — ₹10,000? ₹50,000? This is the first question almost every new seller asks us. The honest answer is that there is no fixed number. What matters is how you use the money you have. In this guide, our founder Krishna M V explains a simple rule that keeps your personal life safe and your stock always ready: the 50% rule.

If you are planning to start an Amazon business with a small budget, read this before you buy any stock.

Rule 1: Use Only Money You Can Afford to Risk

Before you start, decide how much money you can put into the business without touching your personal life. Not your rent. Not your family's savings. Not money you will need next month.

Every new business takes time to learn. If you start with money you cannot afford to lose, every slow week feels like a crisis, and you make rushed decisions. When the money is truly spare, you can stay calm, learn and improve.

That amount is your starting point. Whether it is small or big, this is 100% of your starting budget.

Take Just Half: The 50% Rule

Now split your starting amount into two equal parts:

Part Share What you do with it
Product money 50% Invest only this in your first stock
Reserve money 50% Don't touch it — keep it to refill stock

Only the first half goes into products. The second half stays aside. Most new sellers do the opposite: they spend everything on the first batch of stock and have nothing left when orders finally come in.

Example: How the 50% Rule Works

Let's keep the numbers very simple, as Krishna does in the video.

Say you have ₹100 as your starting amount.

  1. Take just half: only ₹50 is for the product.
  2. With that ₹50, you want to start with 100 products.
  3. Divide: ₹50 ÷ 100 = 50 paise per product.

So your product cost must be 50 paise or less per piece. That is your target cost per product.

In real life, your starting amount may be ₹20,000 or ₹2,00,000 instead of ₹100. The method stays exactly the same: half for stock, divide by the number of pieces you want, and you get the product cost you can afford.

Pick a Product That Fits Your Cost

Once you know your target cost per product, compare your product ideas against it:

  • Product A — over your cost? Drop it for now.
  • Product B — over your cost? Drop it too.
  • Product C — fits your cost? This is the one to start with.

Do not stretch your budget for a product you love. Starting with a product that fits your numbers lets you learn how Amazon works without risking your personal money. You can move to bigger products once you have sales and experience.

Before you fix your final price, also plan for Amazon's selling fees, shipping, packaging and GST, so that the product still leaves you a profit. Check Amazon's latest fee page for your category, because fees change.

Why Keep Half? Never Go Out of Stock

Here is where the second 50% matters.

Your stock starts at 100%. Orders come in, and the stock drops — 41%, then 7%. If you have no money left at this point, the product goes out of stock. Buyers who were ready to order cannot find it, and you lose the sales you worked hard to get.

Orders come in? Refill the stock. The money you kept aside is exactly for this moment. With it, you refill your stock back to 100% and keep selling.

That's why you keep the extra half. This part is important: never go out of stock, especially in your early months when every order helps.

Common Mistakes When Setting Your Amazon Budget

From our work with new sellers, these are the budget mistakes we see most often:

  • Spending everything on the first stock. It feels like more stock means more sales, but with no money left you cannot restock when orders arrive.
  • Using personal or borrowed money. If the business needs time, the pressure falls on your family. Start only with money you can afford to risk.
  • Picking a product above your cost. A costly product means fewer pieces, and running out of stock comes faster.
  • Forgetting the other costs. Product cost is not the only cost. Selling fees, shipping, packaging and GST all come out of your price.
  • Too many variants at the start. Five colours and four sizes split a small budget into tiny lots. Start with one or two variants that fit your budget, and add more once they sell.

Avoiding these five mistakes is often the difference between a seller who quits in the first three months and one who keeps going.

Then Spend Time on Learning

Money is only one part of starting on Amazon. Once your budget is set, spend your time learning how to list, price and grow your product. Frequently asked questions

How much investment do I need to start on Amazon?

There is no fixed number. Start with money you can afford to risk — money that will not affect your personal life. That amount is your starting point, whatever it is.

Can I start selling on Amazon with a small investment?

Yes. The size of your budget decides the price of the product you pick, not whether you can start. A small budget simply means a lower-cost product.

Why should I keep half of my money aside?

To refill stock when orders come in. If your product goes out of stock, you miss sales just when buyers start finding you. The kept half makes sure you can restock quickly.

How do I choose a product for my budget?

Use half your budget for stock, decide how many pieces you want to start with, and divide. The answer is your target cost per piece. Pick a product that fits that cost.

Is product cost the only expense on Amazon?

No. Also plan for Amazon's selling fees, shipping, packaging and GST. Check Amazon's latest fee page for your category before you fix your price.
